# The paper

*Does a tyre-degradation model transfer? Cross-championship evidence and a
retrospective audit of 1,280 real pit-stop decisions.*

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| [`main.tex`](main.tex) | The manuscript. **Contains no digits of its own.** |
| `numbers.tex` | Generated by [`../scripts/make_paper_numbers.py`](../scripts/make_paper_numbers.py) — every quantity the paper quotes, as a LaTeX macro. |

## Why the manuscript has no numbers in it

A paper is the worst place for this project's most persistent defect. Prose does
not recompute, and a submitted PDF cannot be quietly corrected. Over the course
of this work, published prose has said 152 editions after 153 loaded, quoted a
within-stint R² of −6.330 beneath a table reading −1.490, attributed a model's
own P(best) to a team's decision, and named the wrong circuit as the least
trustworthy in a report whose whole purpose was flagging untrustworthy circuits.

So `main.tex` says `\NDecisions`, and `scripts/make_paper_numbers.py` decides
what that means by reading the committed artifacts. Regenerate the artifacts,
regenerate the macros, and the manuscript is correct — or the drift workflow
fails. There is no path where the repository moves and the paper silently
does not.

`tests/test_paper_numbers.py` enforces the arrangement without needing LaTeX
installed: every macro used must be defined, no bare number may appear in a
claim, every included figure must exist, every citation must resolve, and no
bibliography entry may go uncited.

## Building it

There is no LaTeX toolchain in this repository. Compile on
[Overleaf](https://overleaf.com) or locally:

```bash
python scripts/make_paper_numbers.py     # refresh numbers.tex first
cd paper && pdflatex main.tex && pdflatex main.tex
```

Two passes, because of the cross-references. Figures are read from
`../reports/figures/`, so keep the directory structure when uploading — or
run `pdflatex` from this directory rather than copying files elsewhere.

## The bibliography is short on purpose

It holds **only references whose title, authors and venue were checked against
the actual publication record.** Two anchors:

- **Aguad & Thraves (2024)**, *EJOR* 319(3):908–919 — the Stackelberg
  dynamic-programming treatment of the F1 pit stop. The closest existing work to
  this project's adversarial component, and the framing we reuse rather than
  reinvent.
- **Cappello & Hoegh (2025)**, arXiv:2512.00640 — Bayesian state-space tyre
  degradation from FastF1. The closest work on data and method, and the one that
  defines the gap: their evaluation is **a single race**, and they state
  explicitly that generalising across races or circuits is future work with no
  empirical evidence offered. They also report compound-specific degradation
  differences as not statistically distinct, which independently echoes the
  instability this paper measures at scale.

Further work exists that is relevant and **not yet cited because it is not yet
verified**: discrete-event race simulation in *JORS*, a dynamic-programming
treatment in *Central European Journal of Operations Research* 31(1):239–268,
learning-based and multi-agent strategy formulations on arXiv, and explainable
tyre-energy prediction from team-internal telemetry (ACM SAC 2025,
arXiv:2501.04067). Each needs its authors and venue confirmed against the
publication record before it goes into the bibliography. A citation to a paper
that does not exist as described would do more damage than the missing citation.

## Publication route

**Zenodo first**, because it needs nothing from anybody: a real DOI, dated and
citable, and it forecloses neither arXiv nor a journal submission.

arXiv needs an endorsement in the relevant category. A serious reply from any of
the authors above is the realistic route to one, which is what
[`../outreach/`](../outreach/) exists for — and the three questions in
[`../outreach/questions.md`](../outreach/questions.md) are the ones worth
asking them, particularly the second, on whether the audit compares comparable
things.
